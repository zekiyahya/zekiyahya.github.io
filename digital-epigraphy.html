Cuneiform on the Surface, Metadata Beneath: A Two-Layer Web Artwork and the Limits of Linguistic Resistance

Author: Zeki Yahya Year: 2026 Medium: HTML5 / Unicode cuneiform

Abstract. Digital Epigraphy is an experimental web artwork. Its Sumerian-script edition presents Unicode cuneiform characters to human visitors while declaring the language code sux (ISO 639-3) in its markup. The accompanying research article inventories the page into a visible cuneiform surface and a machine-readable substrate, examines SUX Markup, an allegorical notation inspired by the Cylinders of Gudea, and reports exported Google Search Console data (42 clicks and 255 impressions over 92 days; every click attributed to a query came from a search for the author’s name). It finds that the substrate is written in English and enriched with structured data linking the author to more than thirty external identifiers, that Google does not use the language declaration to detect page language, and that the page therefore cannot be said to resist algorithmic data capture. The work is best understood as a double address: a surface that estranges human readers and a substrate that remains fully legible to machines.

Keywords: digital epigraphy; Sumerian; cuneiform; media archaeology; critical code studies; esoteric markup; structured data; surveillance capitalism; web art

1. The project in brief

The web is organised around legibility: pages are written to be parsed, indexed and linked by machines. Digital Epigraphy asks what happens when the visible text of a page is withdrawn from ordinary reading while the page itself stays fully legible to machines. It has two parts: the Sumerian-script edition of the author’s portfolio website, in which headings, statements and film titles appear as strings of Unicode cuneiform characters, and SUX Markup, an allegorical notation in which four cuneiform signs stand for four HTML elements.

2. Surface and substrate

The edition has two layers. Its surface is what a human visitor sees: cuneiform characters laid over a photographic portrait, in a dark, widely spaced layout meant to slow reading. Its substrate is what machines read. The table summarises the principal elements.

A visitor sees one page; a crawler reads another. The visible layer is not a cipher: a cipher hides a message, while this page hides nothing and ornaments the place where a message would ordinarily be read.

3. SUX Markup: a paper architecture

SUX Markup proposes a vocabulary of four signs for the skeleton of a document. The allegory is inspired by the temple-building narrative of the Cylinders of Gudea, which describe the construction of a temple to the god Ningirsu from its foundation upward: a document, like a temple, has a foundation, a head, a house and bricks.

This is a notation, not a working language of the web. Browsers parse HTML and do not recognise cuneiform characters as element names. As XML the example is well-formed under libxml2-based parsers, which implement XML 1.0 (Fifth Edition) and allow supplementary-plane characters in element names, although Python’s built-in expat parser rejects it. A SUX Markup document could therefore be transformed into HTML by a script or stylesheet; no converter is part of the project at present. The live edition is ordinary HTML5 with cuneiform text content.

4. Findings

Search visibility

Search Console data for the exact page /sux/ (search type Web) cover 92 days, from 11 August to 10 November 2026: 42 clicks, 255 impressions, an average click-through rate of 16.47 percent and an average position of 13.27. Clicks and impressions were concentrated in August and September; the last click was recorded on 27 September.

  • All 39 clicks attributed to a query came from three searches that name the author: “zeki yahya” (20), “director zeki yahya” (11) and “filmmaker zeki yahya” (8). The page was found by people already searching for his name.
  • Eight queries consisting of a single cuneiform sign produced 58 impressions and no clicks: the crawler indexed the cuneiform characters as ordinary text and matched them to queries.
  • 37 of the 39 attributed clicks came from Iraq. Search Console does not separate the author’s own searches from those of other people.

There is no control page, so the figures are descriptive only: they neither support nor contradict any claim that the language declaration attracts or repels a system.

The resistance claim, tested

  • Google determines page language from visible content and does not use the HTML lang attribute; its hreflang guidance specifies two-letter ISO 639-1 codes, which sux is not.
  • The substrate is English and richly structured, and links the author to more than thirty external identifiers.
  • Tracking depends on identifiers and behaviour, not on the language of the text. The page requests a font stylesheet from a third party and is hosted on a centralised service.

The work therefore does not resist algorithmic data capture in any technically meaningful sense. It does the opposite of obfuscation: it adds precise, cross-referenced identifiers, and may be described as disambiguation under a mask.

What the work does

The cuneiform surface introduces friction into reading and makes the conventions of a portfolio page, the name, the biography, the navigation, perceptible again. The effect is local rather than total: the portrait, the card layout and the link positions remain conventional. The work also makes visible the indifference of web protocols: Unicode encodes cuneiform, ISO 639-3 registers Sumerian, BCP 47 and ISO 15924 allow a script to be tagged, and HTML accepts all of them without distinction.

“Transferring the melancholic stillness of human transience from the screen to the digital canvas: code re-imagined not as script, but as a monument carved in digital stone.” Artist’s statement

5. Status and limits

  • The strings of cuneiform signs on the edition carry no transliteration or translation and are not offered as verified Sumerian text; no philological review has been carried out.
  • The sign-to-element assignments are symbolic. The assignment of TE to the idea of a foundation has not been verified against a standard sign list, and line-level attestations from the Cylinders are still to be documented.
  • The cuneiform edition has no transliteration layer and is not available to screen readers. The English edition presents the same portfolio in readable form.
  • The study is a practice-based self-case study, with no control condition, partial query-level data and no user study.
  • The project is not a privacy tool and not a functional markup language for browsers.
